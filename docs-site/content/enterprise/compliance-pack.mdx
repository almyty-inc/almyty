# Compliance pack

Make safety filters mandatory for every agent in your organization, and get a report you can hand to an auditor. For example: credit card numbers and email addresses never appear in your logs or in answers to outside systems, and any risky request is blocked, not just logged.

almyty has two built-in safety filters that any agent can use:

- **PII filter**: hides personal data (PII, personally identifiable information) such as email addresses, phone numbers, credit card numbers and social security numbers, wherever it is kept or leaves almyty: the record of each tool call, the steps a run shows under **Runs** (a workflow run's once it has finished), the audit log, and the answers a tool gives to an outside caller through a gateway. It does not change what a tool is called with: a tool that looks up a calendar or searches mail gets the real address, or it would look up the wrong thing.
- **Security scanner**: checks requests, responses and tool inputs for attack patterns (such as attempts to inject database commands or scripts) and acts on what it finds above a severity you choose.

Without the compliance pack each agent opts in on its own. The compliance pack, on the **Business** and **Enterprise** plans, turns them into an organization-wide rule. It is the practical side of the [Compliance](/compliance) page: regulators want controls that apply everywhere, not good intentions agent by agent.

## Set the organization policy

Owners and admins do this under **Settings → Advanced → Compliance**.

![Compliance settings showing the default policy and posture report](/screenshots/settings-compliance-enabled.png)

1. Turn **Enforce PII filter** and **Enforce security scanner** on or off.
2. Under **PII categories to mask**, pick which kinds of personal data to hide. Picking none hides all of them.
3. Choose the **Severity threshold** at which the security scanner acts.
4. Turn on **Block on violation** to stop the offending call, or off to only record it.
5. Click **Save compliance policy**.

**Safe by default.** Until an admin saves a policy, both filters are enforced, at medium severity, and violations are blocked. Saving a policy can only relax that, and does so on purpose. The page always shows the policy in effect and whether it is the default.

## The posture report

The same page shows a report you can attach to a compliance file. Pick **Last 7 days**, **Last 30 days** or **Last 90 days** and click **Download JSON** to save it. It contains:

- **The policy in effect** for that period.
- **Enforced controls**: for each filter, whether it is enforced and its settings.
- **Activity** from the audit log: total events, events by action, how many could be scanned, and how often credentials were accessed.
- **Posture score** (0 to 100): a blunt summary. 40 points for each enforced filter and 20 for blocking. Full marks means both filters are enforced and violations are blocked, not merely logged.

## For developers

The policy:

```json
{
  "enforcedPlugins": ["pii-filter", "security-scanner"],
  "securityThreshold": "medium",
  "blockOnViolation": true,
  "piiCategories": []
}
```

| Field | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `enforcedPlugins` | `pii-filter`, `security-scanner` | which built-in guardrails are mandatory org-wide |
| `securityThreshold` | `low` \| `medium` \| `high` \| `critical` | severity at which the security scanner acts |
| `blockOnViolation` | boolean | block the offending call, or log and continue |
| `piiCategories` | string[] | PII categories to filter; empty means all |

Reading the policy back returns the effective policy and whether it is the default or configured. The report defaults to the last 30 days; the endpoint and its date range are in the [API reference](/api-reference/authentication).
