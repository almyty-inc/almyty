# A refund assistant with safeguards

For teams that want an assistant to handle refunds but can't accept "the AI decided to". This example layers four safeguards on one assistant: firm rules it can't argue with, a second AI model that checks every answer, a person who approves large refunds, and a record of everything it did.

In the demo, the Customer Support Orchestrator stopped on a real case: Brightway Logistics asked for an $820 refund on order NW-44120, which arrived defective. That is over the $500 limit, so the assistant asked for approval and waited.

![An $820 refund waiting for a person to approve it](/screenshots/approval-refund-820.png)

For a full walk-through with screenshots, see [Customer support assistant](/examples/customer-support-assistant).

## What you need

- Your order system connected as an API, with "look up order" and "issue refund" operations.
- Someone who can approve refunds (an owner or admin of your organization).

## How to build it

1. **Write the firm rules.** On the assistant's **Constraints** tab, add rules such as "Refunds over $500 need a person to approve them first" and "Look the order up before answering anything about it." Constraints go with every request, whatever the conversation says.

   ![The support assistant's constraints](/screenshots/support-agent-constraints.png)

2. **Have a second model check.** On its **Overview** tab, under **Models & verification**, click **Configure verification**, turn on **Enable verification** and add reviewers, ideally from a different AI company than the main model. Each reviewer tries to find what is wrong with the answer; with **All pass**, the answer is revised until every reviewer passes it, up to the number of **Max revise loops**. The demo uses an "Accuracy" reviewer that checks order facts against what the tools returned, and a "Policy" reviewer that checks the refund limit.

   ![The support assistant's models and reviewers](/screenshots/support-agent-overview.png)

3. **Put a person on the large refunds.** Tell it in its **Instructions**, as well as in the constraint: "Ask for approval before any refund over $500." When it asks, the run pauses and the request appears on the **Approvals** page, where an owner or admin clicks **Approve** or **Reject**. Below the limit it goes ahead on its own. To make the limit hold even if the assistant forgets, add an amount rule, *Ask before “Create refund” when amount is over 500*, under **Settings → Advanced → Approvals**: almyty then checks every refund call before it is made. See [ask before a large amount](/approvals#ask-before-a-large-amount-whatever-the-assistant-was-told).
4. **Keep the record.** Every action and every change to the assistant is in the audit trail (**Analytics**, **Audit trail**), with who, when and what.

The assistant asks for approval because its instructions tell it to. Keep the rule short and put it in both places.
