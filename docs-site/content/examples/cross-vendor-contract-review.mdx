# Contract review by several AI models

For legal, purchasing and due-diligence teams, where one AI model's reading isn't enough. Several models from different companies each read the contract from their own angle, and a final "judge" model writes one risk memo from what they found. A missed auto-renewal or indemnity clause gets caught by the model that was looking for it.

For example: one model reads as your company, one as the other side, one checks the governing law. The memo lists the risks all of them agreed on.

## What you need

- At least two AI providers connected (for example one from OpenAI and one from Mistral), under **Models**, **Connect a provider**.
- Optionally, a search tool over your clause library or past contracts.

## How to build it

1. **Create the assistant.** Open **Agents**, click **Create agent** and switch to **Autonomous**. In **Instructions**, write: "Review the contract and write a risk memo."
2. **Set the work mode to Panel.** In the **Work mode** section, pick **Panel**. You then fill in the main model, two or more panelists and, if you like, a judge. Give each panelist a different provider, and under its **Advanced**, an angle in its extra instructions: "your company's risk", "the other side's leverage", "compliance with the governing law". A panelist can also be one of your other assistants, with its own tools.

   ![Work mode set to Panel, with panelists on different providers and a judge](/screenshots/agent-work-mode-panel.png)

3. **Add a judge.** The judge reads what the panelists said and writes the one answer they agree on. Without a judge, the main model does it.
4. **Optionally give it a search tool** under **Tools and APIs**, so its reading can point to earlier contracts, not just the text in front of it.
5. **Send it the contract.** Add a **Web chat** channel on its **Channels** tab, publish it, and attach the contract as a PDF, or have your document system call it.
6. **Read the memo.** Each run shows every panelist's answer, which model gave it, and how far they agreed.

## Ideas

- The same setup works for research summaries, due diligence or incident reviews: anywhere one model isn't enough.
- For large volumes of low-stakes work, use **Cascade** instead: a cheaper model answers first, and the main model steps in only when a checker rejects the draft.
- Have it ask a person before anything above a risk level goes out (see [Approvals](/approvals)).
