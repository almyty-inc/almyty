# Decide who signs off on refunds

## What you'll build

Sign-off rules for the refunds an assistant makes in Stripe, set up for Velora Energy, a made-up energy supplier:

- A refund over **€500** waits until the **lead of the Finance team** approves it.
- A refund over **€5,000** needs the Finance lead **and then an admin**, two different people, in that order.
- Smaller refunds go through on their own.

Here a €6,240.00 refund for a customer who was charged twice went through after Joost (Finance lead) and Lotte (admin) both approved:

![The run: the approval request, then the refund in Stripe after both approvals](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/00-two-step.png)

Rules with several steps, or with a particular approver, are **approval policies** and need the **Business** plan. Without it, one approval from any owner or admin decides every request. The screenshots come from a test installation, so web addresses in them start with `http://localhost`. It takes about 15 minutes.

## What you need

- The Business plan, and **owner or admin** in your almyty organization.
- The teams and the **Billing assistant** from [Keep each team's assistants to that team](/examples/multi-team-rbac): a **Finance** team with Joost as its **Lead**, an admin (Lotte), and a billing assistant for Finance that uses Stripe's **List all customers**, **List all charges** and **Create a refund**.

## 1. Tell the assistant when to ask

An approval policy decides **who** signs off. **When** to ask comes from the assistant: it asks with its built-in `request_approval` step, and the policy reads the amount from that request. So the instructions must say when to ask and how to state the amount.

Open the billing assistant, click **Edit** and set **Instructions** to:

> You help Velora Energy's finance team with customers' bills. Find the customer in Stripe by the email address on their account, and look at their charges before you answer.
>
> You can refund a charge in Stripe. Before any refund over €500, ask for approval first with request_approval: in the reason, say who the customer is, which charge, how much and why. In the payload, put the amount in euros as amount_eur, and the customer, charge and invoice. Only make the refund once it is approved. Never refund more than was charged.
>
> Amounts in euros with two decimals.

Click **Save**.

`amount_eur` is a name you choose; the policies below look for exactly that name. Stripe itself counts in cents (a refund of `624000` is €6,240.00), which is why the instructions ask for euros in the request: people reading the request and the rules you write both use euros.

## 2. Two steps for refunds over €5,000

Open **Settings**, then **Advanced → Approvals**. Under **Approval policies**, click **New policy** and fill in:

- **Name**: `Refunds over €5,000`
- **Description**: `Large refunds: the finance lead checks the customer and the charge, then an admin signs off.`
- **Which agents it applies to**: **One team**, **Finance**.
- Under **Match conditions**, click **Add condition**: `payload.amount_eur`, **greater than**, `5000`.
- Under **Approval steps**, the first step: name `Finance lead`, approver role `team_lead`, `1`. Click **Add step** for the second: `Admin`, `admin`, `1`.
- Open **Advanced** and set **Priority** to `10`.

![The policy: refunds over €5,000 need the Finance lead, then an admin](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/01-big-refunds.png)

Click **Create policy**.

The approver role is typed, not picked. These work: `team_lead` (a lead of the team the assistant belongs to), `team_member`, `owner`, `admin`, `member`, and `*` for anyone who may approve.

## 3. One step for refunds over €500

Click **New policy** again:

- **Name**: `Refunds over €500`
- **Description**: `Every other refund that needs a person: the Finance team's lead decides.`
- **Which agents it applies to**: **One team**, **Finance**.
- **Match conditions**: `payload.amount_eur`, **greater than**, `500`.
- **Approval steps**: `Finance lead`, `team_lead`, `1`.

Leave **Priority** at `0` and click **Create policy**.

![Both policies on the Approvals settings page](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/02-policies.png)

A €6,240 refund matches both policies. The one with the higher priority decides, so it gets the two steps. A refund of €740 only matches the second.

## Try it

Open the billing assistant and, under **Try it**, send:

```
Bakkerij De Zon B.V. (administratie@bakkerijdezon.example) was charged twice for their September bill. Please refund the duplicate.
```

The assistant finds the customer and both charges of €6,240.00 for invoice VE-INV-2026-09-20417, and asks for approval before refunding the second one. The run pauses:

![Try it: waiting for approval](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/03-try.png)

**Joost signs first.** As the Finance lead, he is notified (the bell at the top right, and by email), and so are the owners and admins. On **Approvals** he sees the assistant's reason and, under **What it will do**, the charge, the invoice, the customer and the amount it put in the request:

![The refund waiting on the Approvals page, as Joost sees it](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/04-waiting.png)

He clicks **Approve**, adds a note (`Checked in Stripe: the September invoice was collected twice.`) and clicks **Approve** again. The request stays on the page as **pending**: his step is done and the admin's is next. The page does not say which step a request is on, so tell your approvers which requests take two signatures.

![After Joost approved: still pending, now for an admin](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/05-after-first.png)

**Lotte signs second.** She approves on her **Approvals** page. Now both steps are done, the run carries on, and the assistant makes the refund in Stripe:

> Refunded **€6,240.00** to **Bakkerij De Zon B.V.** (VE-B-20417). It was approved first, as refunds over €500 need.
>
> - Stripe refund: `re_3QvD011bBakkerijSe` (succeeded)
> - Charge: `ch_3QvC1bBakkerijSep02`, Invoice VE-INV-2026-09-20417 (September 2026)

The run's steps (picture at the top) show the request for approval followed by the call to **Create a refund**.

**A smaller one.** Send `Anna Kowalski (anna.kowalski@mailbox.example) was billed €1,124.00 on an estimated meter reading. Her actual reading gives €383.40. Refund €740.60.` This matches only **Refunds over €500**: Joost's approval alone lets it through.

![A €740.60 refund: one approval, from the Finance lead](/screenshots/examples/approval-policies/06-small.png)

If anyone clicks **Reject**, the refund is not made and the run stops. A request nobody decides expires after 24 hours.

## Make it yours

- **A quorum.** "Any two people from Finance": one step with role `team_member` and `2`.
- **Other actions.** The same works for anything the assistant asks about, for example credit notes or contract changes: tell it what to put in the request, and match on that.
- **A limit that does not depend on the instructions.** A policy only governs requests the assistant raises. To hold every **Create a refund** call over an amount whatever the assistant does, add an amount rule under **Ask before large amounts** on the same page (free on every plan; one approval decides it). See [ask before a large amount](/approvals#ask-before-a-large-amount-whatever-the-assistant-was-told).

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| A large refund needed only one approval | The assistant left `amount_eur` out of its request, or wrote it as text. On **Approvals**, **What it will do** shows what the request carries (as **Amount eur**); the instructions must name the field exactly. |
| Joost cannot approve | `team_lead` means a lead of the team the **assistant** belongs to. Check that the billing assistant is set to **One team: Finance** and that Joost's role there is **Lead**. |
| Lotte's approval did not finish it | One person counts once per request. Steps go in order: an approval for step two only counts once step one is done. |
| A role name such as `finance` never matches | Approver roles are the built-in names above; custom role names are not used here. |
| **New policy** is missing | Approval policies need the Business plan. |
