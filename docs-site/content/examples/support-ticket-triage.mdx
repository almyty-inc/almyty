# Sort support tickets and draft replies

For support teams with too many routine tickets and no room for a wrong answer. The assistant reads a ticket, looks up the order, and drafts a reply in your voice. A second AI model checks the draft against what the tools actually returned before it goes anywhere, and refunds over your limit go to a person.

For a full walk-through with screenshots, see [Customer support assistant](/examples/customer-support-assistant).

The screenshots here show the demo organization's Customer Support Orchestrator on almyty's local demo setup, with made-up orders and test runs, not a real support account.

![The Customer Support Orchestrator: its models and reviewers](/screenshots/support-agent-overview.png)

## What it does

- *"Where is order NW-10428? The customer says it is late."* It tracks the shipment, finds the parcel held at customs in Leipzig, and drafts an apology with the new delivery date and your delayed-shipment offer.
- *"Triage ticket T-5521 and draft a reply."* It sets the priority, routes the ticket and drafts a reply from your saved replies.
- *"Brightway Logistics wants a refund of $820 on NW-44120."* That is over the $500 limit, so it asks for approval and waits for a person.

![The demo assistant's runs](/screenshots/support-agent-runs.png)

## What you need

- Your order system and your help desk, connected as APIs.

## How to build it

1. **Connect your systems.** Open **APIs**, click **Connect an API**, pick its kind, give the description link and click **Connect API**. Do this for the order system and the help desk; each operation, such as "track shipment" or "reply to ticket", becomes a tool.
2. **Create the assistant.** Open **Agents**, click **Create agent** and switch to **Autonomous**. In **Instructions**, write the job: "Look orders up before answering, never promise a date the carrier hasn't given, and ask a person to approve any refund over $500." Under **Tools and APIs**, pick the two APIs.
3. **Choose how it works.** In **Work mode**, the demo uses **Cascade**: a cheaper model drafts, a checker model looks for mistakes, and the main model steps in only when the checker rejects the draft.
4. **Add reviewers.** On its **Overview** tab, under **Models & verification**, click **Configure verification**, turn on **Enable verification** and add reviewers from different AI companies. The demo has an "Accuracy" reviewer (every order fact must match the tool results) and a "Policy" reviewer (refunds over $500 go to approval), both must pass, with up to two revisions.
5. **Add firm rules.** On its **Constraints** tab, the demo has three: never quote a delivery date the carrier hasn't confirmed, refunds over $500 need a person first, and look the order up before answering.

   ![The demo assistant's constraints](/screenshots/support-agent-constraints.png)

6. **Put it where tickets arrive.** On its **Channels** tab, add Slack, a web chat or email. Or have your help desk send tickets to it (see below).

## Ideas

- Turn a good run into a reusable skill with **Promote to skill** on that run.
- Change the refund limit in the instructions and the constraint together.
