# Where people use it

An app is the one place an agent is put in front of people or other systems: on the web or your own website, in Slack or any other chat app, in front of other agents over A2A, or in a terminal or desktop app. Each of those is a place on the app's **Where people use it** tab, added with **Add a place**. You add a place to record *where* a product will ship; you **publish** it as the separate decision to let people actually reach it. There is exactly one of each per app.

The agent's own **Interfaces** tab lists where it is used, read-only, with a link to each place ("Used in: Acme support > Slack"). A gateway an app stood up says **Managed in** that app on its page and links back to the place.

## Which agent answers

An app can carry several agents, and each place chooses which one answers. A place uses the app's default agent unless you point it at a specific one, so a support product with a triage agent and a billing agent can put the billing agent on the billing channel.

If a place names an agent that has since been removed from the app, publishing is refused rather than going live with something you believe is no longer involved.

## Slack: Add to Slack

The Slack page leads with **Add to Slack**. Create a Slack app at api.slack.com/apps, paste its client ID, client secret and signing secret, and publish. The page then shows the redirect URL to add to your Slack app and the install link to share; every workspace that installs it is listed with a revoke action. A single workspace can use a bot token instead, under **Advanced**.

## Publishing another messaging channel

Other channels (Telegram, WhatsApp, and the rest) take their platform credentials on the place itself, so you stay on the app instead of being sent elsewhere to wire up a gateway. Fill in what the platform needs and publish.

![The Slack distribution with its Bot token and Signing secret fields](/screenshots/apps-slack-credentials.png)

Once published, the distribution is live and shows an **Unpublish** action.

![The Slack distribution published and live](/screenshots/apps-slack-live.png)

Publishing is idempotent: publishing again re-syncs the existing channel rather than failing, because the second attempt is usually you reapplying a settings change. **Unpublishing deactivates** the channel rather than deleting it, so taking a product down for an afternoon does not mean re-registering a Slack app afterwards; republishing keeps the same endpoint and credentials.

## On your website

**On your website** puts the app's agent in a chat bubble on a site you already have. Publish it, and its page gives you one line to paste before the closing `body` tag:

```html
<script src="https://<api-host>/gateways/<gateway-id>/widget.js" async></script>
```

The widget takes its colour, name, greeting, theme and AI disclosure from the app's settings, so it looks the same as the web app. On its page you choose where it sits (bottom right or left) and its launcher icon, with a live preview of the real widget, and list the sites allowed to show it. The widget has no sign-in: anyone on an allowed site can use it, within the app's cost and rate limits (each conversation is a visitor), so it only goes on an app anyone can use. When the app lets visitors, the widget offers *Download my chat* and *Delete my chat*.

## Other agents (A2A)

**Other agents (A2A)** lets agents elsewhere call the app's agent over Google's Agent-to-Agent protocol. Publish it, and its page shows the Agent Card URL and the JSON-RPC endpoint, `https://<api-host>/<org>/apps/<app>/a2a`. Under **How other agents sign in**, make a key for each caller; each call sends it in the `x-api-key` header. Each key has its own share of the app's per-visitor limit, and each task runs under the app's per-run and spend limits. The card and the endpoint answer only while the place is published and its agent is active. See **[A2A](/gateways/a2a)** for the methods and the card.

## What publishing refuses

Publishing turns away three things that would otherwise produce a surface that is live and useless, or open when you meant it closed:

- **Missing credentials**: a channel whose required credentials are absent. Each adapter declares exactly what it uses, so you are told which credential is missing rather than getting a channel that silently receives nothing.
- **A workflow agent behind a chat surface or A2A**: the runtime turns these away at the first message, so publishing stops it up front.
- **The website widget on an app people sign in to**: the widget has no sign-in, so it would be the way round the rule. The widget's page says so before you publish.

## What gates an app from shipping

While you are still editing, the app tells you the unmet requirements continuously, rather than letting you discover them when a publish is rejected. The ones that matter for anything open to the public:

- **A cost ceiling.** Anyone with the link or the binary can spend against your model keys, so an open product needs a per-run ceiling.
- **Rate limits.** One user must not be able to exhaust the product for everyone else. Per-user and per-IP ceilings are separate, because a hosted-chat visitor has no account.
- **No local access on a public artifact.** A downloadable app that touches the machine it lands on cannot also be open to anyone.

An unset auth mode reads as open, not as unset; the permissive reading of missing configuration is the one that gets someone billed. These limits live on the **Settings** tab, under **Advanced**, next to branding and who may use the product. A per-run or rate limit left empty is stored as unset, not as zero.

## What the whole app may spend

On top of the per-run and per-visitor limits, an app has a **spend limit per day** and **per month** for everything it answers, across all of its places and visitors. An app open to anyone starts at 5 a day and 50 a month (UTC days and months); change or clear them on **Settings**, under **Advanced**, where the summary line shows what is set. When a limit is reached, every place tells visitors **This app has reached its limit for today** (or *for this month*) instead of answering, the app's page says so with what it spent, and owners and admins get a notification. See [Protect model spend](/hosted-chat#protect-model-spend) for how each place counts visitors.
