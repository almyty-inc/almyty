# Who can use a credential

You decide who may use each saved key. For example, the refund assistant may use the payments key, but ordinary members cannot, and your own personal key can be lent to one of your assistants without becoming everyone's.

Sharing a [credential](/credentials) with someone is called a grant: one credential, given to one person, team, role, assistant or workspace (a place where an assistant does its work). Admins and owners can use every organization credential already; everyone else needs a grant.

## Share a credential

1. Open **Credentials → Advanced** and pick the credential under **Who can use each credential**. Or open the credential's own page and click **Change** next to **Who can use it**.
2. Under **Principal type**, pick who to share it with: a **User**, a **Team**, a **Role** (Owners, Admins or Members), an **Agent** or a **Workspace**, then pick which one.
3. Under **Permission**, pick **Use** (they may use it) or **Manage** (they may also see and change who else uses it).
4. Optionally set **Expires**, a date after which the grant stops counting.
5. Click **Add grant**.

Remove a grant with the bin icon next to it. A removal takes effect within 30 seconds everywhere.

## Who may use a credential

The first rule that fits decides:

1. The **owner** of a personal credential always may.
2. A credential shared with **one team** can only be used by members of that team (admins and owners excepted).
3. **Admins and owners** may use any organization credential. They cannot use someone's personal credential.
4. Everyone else needs a current grant.

What that means in practice:

- A plain member cannot use an organization credential until it is shared with them. To let every member use one, share it with the role **Members** (viewers can be added through the API, if viewers run assistants).
- Admins and owners use every organization credential without a grant.
- A personal credential is invisible to everyone but its owner until the owner shares it, usually with one of their own assistants.

When a use is refused, the reason is given, and a run that is refused fails with that reason.

## Who may share a credential

| Credential | Person | See grants | Share | Remove a grant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| personal | its owner | yes | yes | yes |
| personal | someone it was shared with to manage | yes | yes | yes |
| personal | an admin or owner | yes | no | yes |
| personal | anyone else | no | no | no |
| organization | an admin or owner | yes | yes | yes |
| organization | someone it was shared with to manage (a team member, for a team credential) | yes | yes | yes |
| organization | anyone else | no | no | no |

So an admin can stop a personal key being shared, without being able to hand it out.

A member can lend their own personal key to their own assistant with no admin involved. Lending it to somebody else's assistant is an admin's decision.

## A record of every use

Every time a run uses a credential, the audit log records who asked (person, assistant, workspace), which credential, which run and why, and on what basis it was allowed. Sharing and removing grants are recorded too. The key itself only goes into the request to the service it belongs to.

Jobs that run without a person (schedules, background upkeep) use organization credentials for the organization. They use a personal credential only if its owner is the one running it, or it has been shared with the run's assistant or workspace.

## For developers

**Principals.**

| Principal | Matches when |
|---|---|
| `user` | The caller is that user |
| `team` | The caller belongs to that team in the credential's organization |
| `role` | The caller holds that organization role (`owner`, `admin`, `member`, `viewer`) |
| `agent` | The use happens inside a run of that agent |
| `workspace` | The use happens inside that workspace |

`user`, `team`, `role` and `agent` are identity-shaped; a `workspace` is a run location. A workspace can be a grant target but never owns a credential.

**Grant budgets.** A grant can also carry a spend budget of its own (`budgetId`), set through the API.

**Permissions.** `use` allows resolving the secret for a run; `manage` adds listing, adding and revoking grants on the credential. Admins and owners hold `connections:manage`, which bypasses team-visibility and grants on organization credentials but does not reach personal ones.

One grant exists per credential and principal; granting again refreshes its permission, budget and expiry in place. An expired grant counts as absent. A grant's own spend budget is stored and returned, and enforced at resolve time by the Enterprise governance module. Deleting the credential removes every grant on it.

Refusal reasons: `no grant on this org-scoped connection`, `no grant on this user-scoped connection`, `not a member of the connection team`. A refused run fails with `CONNECTION_NOT_GRANTED` and the reason.

Granting checks the target: a `user` must be an active member of the organization, a `team` must exist in it, a `role` must be one of the four built-in roles, an `agent` or `workspace` must exist in the organization, and a `budgetId` must belong to it. Without `connections:manage`, an actor may only bind a credential to an agent they created or a workspace they own.

Grants are managed with the same three operations (list, add with principal type and id plus optional permission, budget and expiry, revoke) on the [Credentials API](/api-reference/credentials-api#grants). Granting and revoking write `connection_grant` and `connection_revoke_grant` to the audit log with the principal, permission, budget, expiry and how the actor was authorised. A revoke takes effect at once on the instance that handled it and within 30 seconds on any other; expiry is evaluated on every use.

Each use writes one `connection_resolve` audit row: who asked (user, agent, workspace), which credential, the run and purpose, and how the decision was reached (owner, `connections:manage`, or the grant that carried it). Jobs without a user (schedulers, the deployment reconcile loop) resolve organization credentials for the organization; a personal credential only with its owner as actor, or through a grant to the run's agent or workspace.
