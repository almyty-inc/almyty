# Who can use a credential

A [credential](/credentials) is used by anyone other than its owner only through a grant. A grant hands one credential to one principal: a user, a team, a role, an agent, or a workspace. Grants are what let an autonomous agent run against a key without every member of the organization being able to reach that key, and what let a personal key be lent to one agent without becoming an organization key.

## Principals

| Principal | Matches when |
|---|---|
| `user` | The caller is that user |
| `team` | The caller belongs to that team in the credential's organization |
| `role` | The caller holds that organization role (`owner`, `admin`, `member`, `viewer`) |
| `agent` | The use happens inside a run of that agent |
| `workspace` | The use happens inside that workspace |

`user`, `team`, `role` and `agent` are identity-shaped; a `workspace` is a run location. A workspace can be a grant target but never owns a credential.

## Permissions

| Permission | Allows |
|---|---|
| `use` | Resolving the secret for a run |
| `manage` | Everything `use` allows, plus listing, adding and revoking grants on the credential |

One grant exists per credential and principal; granting again refreshes its permission, budget and expiry in place. A grant may carry an expiry, after which it counts as absent, and a spend budget of its own (stored and returned now; enforced at resolve time by the Enterprise governance module). Deleting the credential removes every grant on it.

## Who may use a credential

In order; the first rule that applies decides:

1. The **owner** of a personal credential always may.
2. A **team-visibility** credential is usable only by members of that team; `connections:manage` bypasses this, as it does elsewhere.
3. **`connections:manage`** (owners and admins) may use any organization credential. It does not reach into personal credentials.
4. Otherwise a matching, unexpired `use` or `manage` grant is needed.

A refusal always says why: `no grant on this org-scoped connection`, `no grant on this user-scoped connection`, `not a member of the connection team`. A run that is refused fails with `CONNECTION_NOT_GRANTED` and the reason.

What follows from the rules:

- A plain member does not use an organization credential until something grants it. To make a credential organization-wide, grant `role: member` (and `role: viewer` if viewers run agents).
- Owners and admins use every organization credential without a grant.
- A personal credential is invisible to everyone but its owner until the owner grants it, typically to one of their agents.

## Who may manage grants

| Credential | Actor | List | Grant | Revoke |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| personal | its owner | yes | yes | yes |
| personal | holder of a `manage` grant | yes | yes | yes |
| personal | `connections:manage` | yes | no | yes |
| personal | anyone else | no | no | no |
| organization | `connections:manage` | yes | yes | yes |
| organization | holder of a `manage` grant (a team member, when team-visibility) | yes | yes | yes |
| organization | anyone else | no | no | no |

An admin can stop a personal secret from being shared without being able to hand it out.

Granting also checks the target. A `user` must be an active member of the organization, a `team` must exist in it, a `role` must be one of the four built-in roles, an `agent` or `workspace` must exist in the organization, and a `budgetId` must belong to it. Without `connections:manage`, an actor may only bind a credential to an agent they created or a workspace they own: a member grants their own personal credential to their own agent with no admin involved, and binding it to somebody else's agent is an admin decision.

## Managing grants

Grants live on the credential. Open **Credentials → Advanced**, pick the credential under **Who can use each credential** (or click **Change** next to **Who can use it** on the credential's own page), and use the editor to list them, add one (a principal type and id, plus an optional permission, budget and expiry) and revoke one; the same three operations are on the [Credentials API](/api-reference/connections-api#grants). Granting and revoking write `connection_grant` and `connection_revoke_grant` to the audit log with the principal, permission, budget, expiry and how the actor was authorised.

A revoke takes effect at once on the instance that handled it and within 30 seconds on any other; expiry is evaluated against the clock on every use.

## What a run sees

When a run uses a credential it records one `connection_resolve` audit row: who asked (user, agent, workspace), which credential, the run and purpose, and how the decision was reached (owner, `connections:manage`, or the grant that carried it). The secret goes only into the request to the provider it belongs to.

Jobs without a user (schedulers, the deployment reconcile loop) use organization credentials for the organization; a personal credential is used for them only with its owner as actor, or through a grant to the run's agent or workspace.
